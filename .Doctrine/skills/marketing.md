# Skill: Marketing

**Scope.** Positioning, messaging, channels, and launches — carrying the
product's true story to the people whose pain it solves. The human sends.

## Principles
- Positioning is one sentence naming the user, the pain, and the
  alternative. If it needs a paragraph, it isn't positioning yet.
- Every channel claim is a hypothesis until measured (`skills/metrics.md`).
  "Works for companies like us" is a prior, not a result.
- A launch is a release: checklist, staged rollout, rollback story — the
  same discipline code gets, because the audience is production too.
- The honesty law covers copy: no invented users, quotes, or figures;
  estimates labeled; every claim traces to a shipped artifact.
- Attention is spent once per reader: one artifact, one CTA.

## Defaults
- Positioning sentence on record and versioned; revisited when the product
  shifts, not when the copy feels stale.
- Launch checklist: assets inventoried, every claim checked against the
  CHANGELOG, comms staged owned → warm → paid, an owner per step.
- The apology draft is written BEFORE the send. If the launch breaks, the
  response gets edited, not composed under fire.
- Channel experiments sized small, success metric and time window declared
  before the first dollar or post.
- Everything external is a draft queued for the human (.Doctrine.md §5):
  posts, emails, ads, replies. The skill never touches the send button.

## Do
- Test the message on warm traffic (existing users, list, community)
  before buying reach; paid spend amplifies whatever conversion already
  exists — including zero.
- Report reach metrics only alongside the metric that pays rent behind
  them: impressions with signups, signups with activation.
- Kill or scale channel experiments on the declared metric at the declared
  window — no "one more week" without a written reason.
- Keep a claims file per campaign: each assertion → the artifact or
  measurement backing it, reviewed before the human sends.

## Don't
- Don't buy reach before the message converts warm traffic.
- Don't report vanity metrics naked — followers without funnel is theater.
- Don't stack CTAs; a reader given two next steps takes neither.
- Don't announce what hasn't shipped; roadmap is not inventory.
- Don't let "everyone" be the audience; positioning that excludes no one
  reaches no one.

## Search & answer-engine optimization (SEO/GEO)
Being found now spans two surfaces: classic search (SEO) and AI answer engines
that synthesize a reply and may cite you (GEO — generative-engine optimization).
Both reward the same thing — genuine, well-structured authority, not tricks.
- Earn the citation, don't game it: answer the real question completely, with a
  direct answer up top and clear structure. Engines lift self-contained, factual
  passages; buried or hedged answers don't get cited.
- Structure for machines and people: semantic HTML, descriptive headings,
  structured data where it fits, fast crawlable pages. SEO and accessibility pull
  the same direction (`skills/accessibility.md`).
- Authority is earned, not injected: original data, real expertise, honest
  references. Keyword-stuffing, cloaking, and link schemes are the honesty law's
  problem — and engines penalize them.
- GEO specifics: be quotable (a crisp, sourced claim a model can lift), keep facts
  current and dated, and make your entity legible (consistent name, clear "what we
  do") so a model resolves and attributes you correctly.
- Measure the traffic that pays rent: rankings and citations are vanity until tied
  to signups/activation (`skills/metrics.md`) — a #1 ranking that converts no one
  is theater.

## Review checklist
- [ ] Positioning fits one sentence: user, pain, alternative
- [ ] Every claim traces to a shipped artifact or labeled estimate
- [ ] One CTA per artifact; funnel metric named beside every reach metric
- [ ] Launch checklist staged; apology draft written before the send
- [ ] Channel experiment has declared metric, window, and spend cap
- [ ] All sends queued as drafts for the human

## Common failure modes
Positioning by committee that names no one; paid spend rescuing a message
warm traffic already rejected; launch-day improvisation because the
checklist was "obvious"; vanity dashboards divorced from revenue; the
quiet slide from "we're building" to "we have" that the honesty law
exists to stop.
